## Claim and scope

The nine-message corpus cannot be generated by

```text
C[t] = P[t] + F(C[t-1]) (mod 83)
```

with a single shared arbitrary lookup table F and a fixed encoded input alphabet of **52 or fewer residues**. Eleven observed predecessor contexts force at least **53** distinct input residues. No language, guessed plaintext, repeated passage, or key is assumed.

This strengthens Chesewip's published 43-residue bound using the same reduction and additional observed contexts. It does not identify the Noita cipher or recover plaintext. The exact optimum for the selected eleven sets is 53; the full-corpus optimum may be larger.

The support count is a count of encoded residues. A direct encoding of the 52 uppercase and lowercase Latin letters with no other tokens is within the excluded size range. Adding spaces, punctuation, homophones, syllables, or other tokens changes that count and must be evaluated under its actual encoding. The bound says nothing about which human language was used.

Here shifts are added to observed ciphertext successors, so they correspond to minus F on these eleven contexts, up to a common translation. The 53-element union is saved in [results/feedback.json](../results/feedback.json). This partial table is an optimization witness, not a full-corpus key.

## Verification

[code/verify_feedback.py](../code/verify_feedback.py) imports no discovery code. It re-derives all context sets and observation locations from the pinned corpus, constructs translations by direct modular addition, and enumerates distinct feasible partial unions breadth-first under the fixed claimed bound.

For eleven sets, the numbers of distinct partial unions of size less than 53 are:

```text
[1, 83, 6889, 384973, 1716039, 604875, 60199, 2531, 64, 1, 0]
```

A hypothetical complete union smaller than 53 would have every partial union smaller than 53, so it would survive every layer. The empty final layer excludes it. Separately, the supplied shift vector is checked to produce exactly 53 residues. Together these prove the selected-subset optimum.

Controls include three planted eleven-set problems with a known 27-residue alphabet, plus 30 small random problems over moduli 3, 5, and 7 checked by exhaustive Cartesian enumeration. Discovery and the separate verifier agree in all 33 cases. These controls check support optimization; they do not claim unknown-key language recovery or a recovered Noita decoder.

The independent verifier's implementation is separate from discovery, but the mathematical reduction and breadth-first verification strategy are credited to the earlier contribution. This is computational replication and extension, not a claim to a new general cryptanalytic method.

All five real-data cases and all 33 controls passed. The Google Doc transcription also matches the corpus at every raw position. Results are in [feedback_verification.json](../results/feedback_verification.json) and [source_check.json](../results/source_check.json).

### Sensitivity and boundaries

Under fixed message boundaries and trigram grouping, substituting e raw trigrams changes at most 2e observed adjacent pairs. Keeping these eleven context labels fixed can remove at most 2e elements from their successor sets. Restoring each removed element can increase a union by at most one. Therefore the corrected-data bound remains at least `53 - 2e`; for one substitution it is at least 51. This does not cover insertions, deletions, or a different reading convention.

An invertible affine relabeling of residues modulo 83 preserves the family and support size, so a global numerical shift or nonzero scaling does not evade the result. An arbitrary nonlinear relabeling is a different question.

No alternate arithmetic or reverse-reading result from the earlier report was rerun here, and none is strengthened by this submission. No full-corpus feedback minimum, authentic plaintext, historical key, or held-out decryption prediction is claimed.
